# 03: Explain revolutionary change through interactive visual scenes

## What to build

Help students understand political changes by manipulating simple visual explanations within the bilingual timeline. Interaction should clarify historical meaning without inventing a score, requiring a quiz, or turning historical outcomes into a game.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The Estates-General scene compares voting by estate with voting by head and explains the Third Estate's representation. Clearly distinguish schematic marks from measured population data.
- [ ] The Bastille scene lets students reveal how its fall challenged royal authority, explaining the difference between its seven prisoners and its symbolic importance. Do not suggest the fortress was instantly demolished.
- [ ] The October march scene distinguishes the march to Versailles on 5 October from the royal family's return to Paris on 6 October. Label any route diagram as schematic unless it uses verified geography.
- [ ] Other moments have relevant explanatory visuals, including limits on monarchy, the change to a republic, tensions between emergency powers and liberty, the five directors and Napoleon's dominance within a three-consul system.
- [ ] Interactive controls update both the visual and its textual explanation, work with keyboard and touch, and have meaningful accessible labels and state.
- [ ] All visual labels and explanations switch between English and French without changing the selected historical moment.
- [ ] Color is not the only way to understand the diagrams, and the main historical explanation remains accessible without motion.
- [ ] Verify the voting comparison, Bastille reveal and march interaction in both languages.
