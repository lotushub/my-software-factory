# 02: Explore all ten turning points from 1789 to 1799

## What to build

Complete the bilingual timeline so a secondary-school student can explore the main events and understand how the revolution changed direction in approximately five minutes. Each event explains what happened and why it matters; no event requires completing an earlier one.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Include ten chronological moments: Estates-General (5 May 1789); Tennis Court Oath (20 June); Bastille (14 July); abolition of privileges and Declaration of Rights (August); women's march (5–6 October); Varennes and constitutional monarchy (1791); monarchy abolished and republic established (1792, with Louis XVI's execution in January 1793 explained); Terror and Robespierre's fall (1793–1794); Directory (1795–1799); Napoleon's coup (9–10 November 1799).
- [ ] Each moment has English and French titles, date or date range, concise explanation, significance, and a relevant source link. Selecting any moment updates the complete event view consistently.
- [ ] An editorial read-through of the principal content takes approximately five minutes; source material and optional detail may take longer.
- [ ] Explain that revolutionary ideals did not give women equal political rights and that colonial slavery contradicted universal claims. Distinguish the 1799 Consulate from the 1804 Empire.
- [ ] Provide a concise bilingual conclusion explaining liberty, equality and national sovereignty, including the tension between ideals and practice.
- [ ] Sources and artwork attribution are discoverable. Historical dates and major claims are checked against credible references; historical quotations are accurately attributed if used.
- [ ] If navigation spaces events equally, disclose that it does not represent elapsed time proportionally.
- [ ] Browser-check direct selection, previous/next navigation and language switching across the first, middle and final events.
